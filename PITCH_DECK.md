## Slide 2 — Origin Story & The Hook

### *The first 30 seconds determine everything*

**Opening line (say this first):**

> *"Imagine you're stopped somewhere. Someone cites a law section you've never heard of. You don't know if it's serious, what your rights are, or what to say. That moment — confusion under pressure — is why Court Companion exists."*

### The story

At some point during travel, I — along with people I know — was stopped and questioned in a way that felt intimidating. The officer used **legal sections, offence numbers, and procedural language** I didn't fully understand.

That moment was confusing and frightening. You just want to resolve the situation — but you don't know:
- What your rights are
- What those law sections actually mean
- How to respond calmly

I'm not making accusations against anyone — many officers serve with integrity. But that experience showed me how easily **lack of legal literacy** leaves ordinary people feeling powerless, even when they've done nothing wrong.

When I saw the **AI for Civic Innovation Hackathon**, it clicked:

> *What if legal rights and procedures were accessible to anyone, in their own language, on their phone — before or during a stressful moment?*

**That's Court Companion.**

## Slide 3 — The Problem

### Who is suffering?

| Group | Situation |
|-------|-----------|
| **General citizens** | Need quick answers about FIR, bail, arrest — no lawyer on call |
| **Rural communities** | Limited access to legal aid, courts, and English resources |
| **Low-literacy users** | Can't parse dense statute language even in Urdu |
| **Regional language speakers** | Pashto, Sindhi, Punjabi, Balochi speakers underserved by English-only legal material |
| **Students & youth** | Curious about rights but no simple, trustworthy source |
| **People under stress** | Stopped, threatened, or filing an FIR for the first time — need clarity *now* |
| **NGOs & legal aid groups** | Can't scale human counseling to millions who need basic awareness |

**Not suffering:** Lawyers and legal professionals — they already have training and access.  
**Court Companion is for everyone else.**

---

### What exactly is the pain?

1. **"I don't know what that section means."**  
   Someone cites PPC §302 or CrPC §154 — citizen has no idea what it implies.

2. **"I don't know my rights."**  
   After arrest, during FIR, at a checkpoint — what can they legally do or say?

3. **"I can't find the answer."**  
   Statutes are hundreds of pages. Google gives random blogs. ChatGPT invents sections.

4. **"Not in my language."**  
   Laws are indexed and written primarily in English. Regional languages are an afterthought.

5. **"I can't afford a lawyer for a simple question."**  
   Basic legal awareness shouldn't cost thousands of rupees per consultation.

6. **"My situation has many parts — which law applies?"**  
   Real cases mix facts + PPC sections + CrPC procedure + rights. A shop theft isn't just "what is theft" — it's cognizable or not, can police delay FIR, which section for ₹150,000, and what steps come next.

**Emotional pain:** Fear · Confusion · Powerlessness · Delayed action · Reliance on rumors

---

### Why does it exist today?

| Root cause | Why it persists |
|------------|-----------------|
| **Laws written for lawyers** | PPC, CrPC, ATA use formal legal English — not citizen-friendly language |
| **No free, trustworthy digital tool** | Keyword search fails on 500-page PDFs; generic AI hallucinates |
| **Lawyer access is expensive & uneven** | Urban bias; rural and low-income citizens left behind |
| **Multilingual gap** | Pakistan has 7+ major languages; legal resources are overwhelmingly English-centric |
| **No Pakistan-specific AI assistant** | Global chatbots don't know PPC §302 from US Code §302 |
| **Stress amplifies the gap** | People search for answers *after* they're already scared — when clarity matters most |

**Bottom line:** The law exists. **Understanding it** doesn't — not for ordinary people, not in their language, not on their phone.

## Slide 4 — The Solution

### Explain it so a 12-year-old understands

**Lead with the outcome — not the technology:**

> **Before:** You're confused. Someone mentions a law. You panic or guess.  
> **After:** You open Court Companion, ask your question in Urdu or English, and get a **simple explanation** of what the law says — with **proof** showing which exact section it came from.

### What Court Companion does (no jargon)

1. **You describe your situation** — type it or say it into your phone.  
   Simple: *"What is an FIR?"*  
   Complex: *"Someone broke into my Lahore shop and stole ₹150,000 — police won't register FIR yet. What section applies and what can I do?"*

2. **It finds the real law** — not a guess. It looks up the actual Pakistani statute (PPC, CrPC, or ATA).

3. **It guides you step by step** — which sections might apply, what procedure the law describes, and what rights or next steps to understand.

4. **It explains in simple words** — in the language you chose: English, Urdu, Pashto, Sindhi, and more.

5. **It shows you the source** — so you can see *where* the answer came from.

6. **It's on your phone** — Android app or web browser. No law degree required.

### One-sentence pitch

**Court Companion is like having a patient friend who read the law books for you — and explains your rights in the language you actually speak.**

### What it is NOT

- Not a lawyer  
- Not legal representation  
- Not a replacement for court  

It is **trustworthy legal information** — fast, plain, multilingual, on mobile.

## Slide 4B — Real-World Scenario Guidance *(not just definitions)*

Court Companion does more than define legal terms. Users can paste a **full real-life story** and get guided answers that connect **facts → PPC sections → CrPC procedure → rights**.

**Pitch line:** *"Tell it what happened. It tells you what the law says — and what to understand next."*

---

### Scenario 1 — Shop theft & FIR dispute (Lahore)

**User story:**

> I live in Lahore. Last week someone broke into my shop at night and stole cash and a laptop. I went to the police station the next morning but the officer said they cannot register an FIR yet because they need to investigate first. Another person told me theft is a cognizable offence and police must record my complaint. If the stolen items are worth around 150,000 rupees, which section of the Pakistan Penal Code might apply, and what is the general procedure under the Criminal Procedure Code for reporting this crime and starting investigation?

**What Court Companion guides on:**

| Topic | Guidance |
|-------|----------|
| **PPC** | Which theft-related sections may apply (e.g. theft, house-breaking) given value and facts |
| **CrPC** | FIR under §154, cognizable vs non-cognizable, police duty to record information |
| **Procedure** | General steps for reporting, investigation start, what complainant can expect |
| **Practical clarity** | Plain explanation of why police may delay vs what the law says about recording complaints |

---

### Scenario 2 — Arrest without warrant & 24-hour rule

**User story:**

> My cousin was arrested yesterday evening from his home without showing any warrant. The police said it is related to a fight that happened three days ago where someone was injured but not killed. He has not been produced before any magistrate yet and it has been more than 24 hours. If the injury was serious but not fatal, can police still hold him without bail, and under which sections of CrPC and PPC should his family understand his rights regarding bail and production before a magistrate?

**What Court Companion guides on:**

| Topic | Guidance |
|-------|----------|
| **PPC** | Hurt / grievous hurt sections relevant to a fight with serious but non-fatal injury |
| **CrPC — arrest** | Arrest without warrant, custody limits, production before magistrate |
| **CrPC — bail** | Bail framework (§496–498 area), when bail may be considered vs when it is harder |
| **Family rights** | What to understand about detention time, magistrate production, and next procedural steps |

---

### Scenario 3 — Section 302, self-defence & bail before trial

**User story:**

> Suppose a person is accused under Section 302 PPC but claims it was self-defence during a robbery at his house at night. The complainant says it was murder, not self-defence. The accused has no previous criminal record and is the only earner for his family. Before the trial starts, what is the difference between qatl-i-amd and ordinary murder under PPC, and can bail even be considered in a 302 case under CrPC, or is it automatically refused in all circumstances?

**What Court Companion guides on:**

| Topic | Guidance |
|-------|----------|
| **PPC — qatl-i-amd** | Difference between qatl-i-amd (§302 area) and related murder concepts in plain language |
| **PPC — self-defence** | General legal ideas around private defence — what statute text covers (not case verdict) |
| **CrPC — bail in 302** | Whether bail is automatically refused in *all* circumstances or may still be considered (with limits) |
| **Before trial** | What family should understand procedurally while case is pending — informational, not legal advice |

## Slide 5 — How It Works

### Simple flow (audience version)

```
You ask a question (any language)
        ↓
Court Companion finds the right law section (PPC / CrPC / ATA)
        ↓
It explains it in simple words — in YOUR chosen language
        ↓
You see the answer + which law section it came from
```

### What happens behind the scenes (technical, one slide)

| Step | What happens |
|------|----------------|
| **1** | User types or speaks a question; picks response language (Urdu, English, Pashto, etc.) |
| **2** | Flutter app sends the question to the live API on Render |
| **3** | If the question isn't in English, it's translated to English **for search only** |
| **4** | The system searches **983 indexed statute chunks** (PPC, CrPC, ATA) using semantic + keyword hybrid search |
| **5** | Top relevant sections are retrieved with metadata (section number, title, document) |
| **6** | An AI model reads those sections and writes a **plain-language answer** in the user's chosen language |
| **7** | Answer streams live to the app — with source chips and a disclaimer |
| **8** | In voice mode, each sentence is spoken as it arrives |

### Two pipelines (important — don't mix them up)

| Pipeline | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **A — Search** | Turn the question into English so the law index can find the right section |
| **B — Answer** | Write the explanation in whatever language the user selected |

The law books are in **English**. The answer can be in **any of 7 languages**.

### Why this design?

- **Retrieve first, explain second** — the AI doesn't guess; it reads real statute text first  
- **Translate for search, not for truth** — English index, multilingual answers  
- **Citations build trust** — every answer shows which section informed it

## Slide 6 — Tech Stack + Knowledge Sources

### Knowledge sources (datasets)

| Statute | File | Coverage | Scale |
|---------|------|----------|-------|
| **Pakistan Penal Code (PPC)** | Act XLV of 1860 | Offences, punishments, qatl/qisas | ~164 pages |
| **Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC)** | Act V of 1898 | FIR (§154), arrest, bail (§496–498), investigation | ~290 pages |
| **Anti-Terrorism Act (ATA)** | Act XXVII of 1997 | Terrorism offences & special procedure | ~46 pages |

**Corpus total:** ~500 pages · ~194,000 words · ~252,000 tokens

**Processed into:**
- **983 section-aware chunks** — custom pipeline preserves section numbers, titles, topics  
- **FAISS vector index** — semantic search over embeddings  
- **`chunks.json`** — metadata per chunk (document, section, title, page)

**What we did NOT use:** No fine-tuning dataset · No user chat logs · No scraped case law · No third-party legal Q&A sets

---

### Technology stack

| Layer | Technology | Role |
|-------|------------|------|
| **Mobile & web app** | Flutter (Dart) | Android APK + Chrome web client |
| **Landing page** | React, Vite, Tailwind | Project site on Vercel |
| **API** | FastAPI (Python) | Legal Q&A, streaming, document analysis |
| **Vector search** | FAISS | Hybrid search over 983 chunks |
| **Embeddings** | `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` | Turn statute text into searchable vectors |
| **Search translation** | Meta Llama 3 8B Instruct Lite | Non-English question → English for FAISS |
| **Answers** | Meta Llama 3.3 70B Instruct Turbo | Plain-language explanations in user's language |
| **LLM provider** | Together.ai | Hosted inference API |
| **API hosting** | Render | HTTPS backend + FAISS index |
| **Voice (input)** | `speech_to_text` | Microphone → text |
| **Voice (output)** | Browser TTS / `flutter_tts` | Spoken streaming answers |

### API endpoints (deployed)

| Endpoint | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| `GET /health` | Index & LLM status |
| `POST /ask` | Legal Q&A |
| `POST /ask/stream` | Streaming answers (NDJSON) |
| `POST /analyze-document` | PDF/TXT case document analysis |

### B. Secondary research

*Public data and established patterns that support the problem — use on the traction slide alongside your survey.*

| Finding | Source / context |
|---------|------------------|
| **~240M population, low lawyer density** | Pakistan has a very low lawyer-to-population ratio compared to developed countries; most citizens cannot access counsel for basic questions ([World Bank / legal access literature](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/justice)) |
| **Legal materials are English-centric** | PPC, CrPC, and ATA are authored in formal English; literacy and language barriers exclude large segments of the population |

| **Generic AI is risky for law** | LLMs hallucinate legal citations without retrieval grounding — documented risk in legal AI research (e.g. Stanford RegLab studies on legal hallucination) |
| **Civic legal literacy gap** | Global "access to justice" gap: billions lack meaningful access to legal information and remedies ([OECD / UNDP access-to-justice frameworks](https://www.undp.org/)) |
| **Mobile-first opportunity** | Pakistan has 100M+ mobile broadband connections — citizens already search for answers on phones; no dominant Pakistan-specific legal AI product exists |
